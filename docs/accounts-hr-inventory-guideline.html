Accounts, HR, and Inventory Management Guideline

This document explains the purpose of the Accounts, HR, and Inventory Management modules in simple operational language, so that an admin, manager, or operator can understand what each module is for and what sequence to follow.

1. Big Picture

Module Main Purpose Used For
Accounts Tracks money movement and financial records Income, supplier dues, payments, purchases, journal entries, fund transfers, reports
HR Tracks employees and workforce operations Departments, designations, shifts, attendance, leave, payroll, documents, notices
Inventory Tracks stock movement and stock health Ledger, low stock monitoring, product stock movement, manual stock adjustment

2. Accounts Module

Accounts module is the financial control center. It stores how money enters the business, leaves the business, and moves between internal accounts.

2.1 Main Sections

  • Account Heads: Financial categories such as expense head, income head, payable head, cash head.
  • Financial Accounts: Actual money holding points such as cash, bank, mobile banking wallet.
  • Income Categories and Income: Different kinds of incoming money and their actual entries.
  • Journal Entries: Manual accounting adjustments for balancing or special bookkeeping entries.
  • Supplier Master: Supplier profile and statement history.
  • Supplier Ledger / Supplier Payments: Supplier balance tracking and payment settlement.
  • Purchases / Purchase Returns: Product procurement and return handling.
  • Customer Dues: Track receivable amounts from customers.
  • Fund Transfer: Move money from one internal financial account to another.
  • Return Refund: Customer refund or reverse payment tracking.
  • Reports: Ledger, daily closing, purchase report, payment method report.

2.2 Suggested Working Sequence

  1. Create Account Heads.
  2. Create Financial Accounts such as cash, bank, bKash, Nagad.
  3. Add Suppliers if purchases will be recorded.
  4. Record Purchases and Supplier Payments.
  5. Record Income, Customer Dues, and Fund Transfers.
  6. Use Journal Entries only when accounting adjustment is needed.
  7. Review reports regularly for reconciliation.

2.3 Practical Meaning

  • If stock is bought from a supplier, it should usually be reflected in both Purchases and supplier-related financial records.
  • If money is received manually outside normal order flow, it can be entered through Income.
  • If cash moves from cash box to bank account, use Fund Transfer.
  • If a customer still owes money, use Customer Due.

2.4 Management Rule

Accounts data should be entered carefully because these records affect reports and operational decisions. Wrong account selection or duplicate entries can distort financial statements.

3. HR Module

HR module is for employee structure, employee records, attendance discipline, salary processing, and personnel administration.

3.1 Main Sections

  • Departments: Team or functional grouping, such as Sales, Warehouse, Accounts, Customer Care.
  • Designations: Job title, such as Manager, Executive, Officer.
  • Shifts: Working schedule, for example 9 AM to 6 PM.
  • Employees: Employee master record with assignment to department, designation, shift, and possibly linked user account.
  • Attendance: Daily attendance entry and dashboard monitoring.
  • Leave Types: Casual leave, sick leave, annual leave, unpaid leave.
  • Leave: Employee leave applications and balance tracking.
  • Salary Structures: Pay setup such as basic, allowance, deduction logic.
  • Payroll: Salary generation, preview, update, and payslip output.
  • Advances: Salary advance or employee advance tracking.
  • Bonuses: Incentive and bonus records.
  • Documents: Employee files such as ID, appointment letter, certificates.
  • Holidays, Notices, Performance Notes, Separation: HR administration and compliance support.

3.2 Suggested Working Sequence

  1. Create Departments, Designations, and Shifts.
  2. Create Employees.
  3. Set up Leave Types and Salary Structures.
  4. Record Attendance daily or in bulk.
  5. Manage Leaves, Advances, and Bonuses during the month.
  6. Run Payroll after attendance and salary rules are ready.
  7. Maintain Documents, Notices, and separation records when needed.

3.3 Practical Meaning

  • Employee is the main master record. Without employee setup, other HR features will not work properly.
  • Attendance and Leave should be kept updated before payroll processing.
  • Salary Structure defines salary logic, while Payroll creates the actual pay record for a period.

3.4 Management Rule

HR data should be entered in order. If employees are created before departments, shifts, or designations are ready, later data cleanup becomes difficult.

4. Inventory Management Module

Inventory module is responsible for stock visibility. It helps track what came in, what went out, current low-stock risks, and any manual correction to stock quantity.

4.1 Main Sections

  • Dashboard: Overview of stock conditions.
  • Inventory Ledger: Full list of stock movements with dates, reference, note, and movement type.
  • Product Movements: Stock history for one product, including variant-wise movement if applicable.
  • Low Stock: Shows products or variants that reached or dropped below threshold.
  • Adjustments: Manual stock increase or decrease for correction purposes.

4.2 Practical Meaning

  • If a product was sold, purchased, returned, or adjusted, the movement should appear in inventory history.
  • Low Stock is used for replenishment planning.
  • Adjustment In means stock is added manually.
  • Adjustment Out means stock is reduced manually.

4.3 Suggested Working Sequence

  1. Ensure products and their stock structure are already configured.
  2. Review Inventory Ledger regularly.
  3. Use Low Stock to identify urgent reorder needs.
  4. Use Adjustment only when physical stock and system stock do not match.

4.4 Management Rule

Inventory adjustment should not be used as a replacement for correct purchase, return, or order recording. Adjustment is mainly for correction, not for normal flow.

5. How These Three Modules Connect

Business Event Related Module Typical Impact
Product purchase from supplier Accounts + Inventory Stock increases, supplier payable or payment record is created
Supplier payment Accounts Supplier due reduces, financial account balance changes
Employee salary processing HR + Accounts Payroll is prepared and later may affect expense accounting
Wrong stock counted physically Inventory Stock adjustment corrects the quantity
Customer refund Accounts Return refund record is stored and financial balance may reduce

6. Simple Admin Checklist

  • Before using Accounts, define account heads and financial accounts.
  • Before using HR, define department, designation, and shift.
  • Before relying on Inventory, verify product stock setup and low-stock threshold.
  • Do not use manual adjustment if a normal operational flow already exists.
  • Use reports weekly to catch mismatches early.

7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Creating financial entries without selecting the correct account head.
  • Running payroll before attendance and leave are finalized.
  • Using stock adjustment for normal purchase flow instead of purchase entry.
  • Entering duplicate supplier or employee master records.
  • Skipping daily reconciliation and discovering mismatch too late.
Recommended training approach: First teach the user what each module means, then teach the sequence, then provide 2-3 real examples such as supplier purchase, employee salary, and stock correction.
